Dr. Ilayda Altuntas

Project Creator, Principal Investigator, and Artist-Researcher

Dr. Ilayda Altuntas is an artist, researcher, and Assistant Professor of Art & Visual Culture Education at the University of Arizona. Her work brings together sound, listening, environmental experience, and artmaking to explore relationships among place, memory, ecology, and material environments through sensory experience and creative practice.

As the creator and principal investigator of Resonant Ecologies, Dr. Altuntas developed the project's conceptual framework, research methodology, field recordings, photography, and audiovisual works. Through listening, field recording, and experimental audiovisual translation, she investigates environmental resonance across Southern Arizona, transforming sound and material vibrations into abstract geometric forms shaped by rhythm, movement, pulse, and distortion.

Altuntas also developed the project's artistic direction, visual identity, and initial website design, including its map-based navigation, organization of recordings into air, land, and water, and presentation as a publicly accessible digital archive and interactive sound map.

Yuchun Zhang

Web Developer and Digital Cartography Collaborator

Yuchun Zhang is an urban designer, researcher, and web mapping designer whose work connects spatial analysis, GIS-based cartography, and interactive digital interfaces. With a background in architecture and urban design, she uses geospatial mapping and web visualization to examine relationships among landscape, infrastructure, environmental systems, and lived experience.

For Resonant Ecologies, Zhang contributed to the technical development of the website and its interactive watershed-based mapping interface, translating the project's initial design concepts into a functioning digital platform. Her cartographic contribution situates the five recording sites within the broader hydrological context of Southern Arizona through geospatial data, river systems, washes, and watershed boundaries.

Across her broader practice, Zhang uses mapping, spatial research, and digital interfaces to explore questions of belonging, memory, care, ecology, resilience, and collective space.
